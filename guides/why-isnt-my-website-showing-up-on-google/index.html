Why isn't my tradie website showing up on Google?

The Google Business Profile vs. website mix-up that trips up most trade businesses, explained plainly.

Here's the short of it

Why this usually happens

Usually it's one of a few things — the site was never submitted to Google, it's not properly linked to your Google Business Profile, it's missing schema markup that tells Google what your business actually does and where, or there's simply too little content for Google to know what to rank it for. Most of this is fixable without touching the design.

The gist

Key takeaways

✓Having a website and showing up on Google are two different things — a site can exist and still be invisible to search.
✓Google Business Profile and your website do different jobs, and confusing them is the most common reason a business thinks their site is "working" when it isn't.
✓Schema markup and submitting your sitemap to Search Console are basic steps that get skipped more often than you'd expect.
✓A site with one page and no local content gives Google very little to rank you for.
✓Fixing this is usually a setup problem, not a design problem.

The site was never submitted to Google

Google doesn't automatically know a new website exists. It needs to be submitted through Google Search Console, along with a sitemap — a file listing every page on the site. Skip this step and a site can sit online for months without being indexed at all, regardless of how good it looks.

It's not linked properly to your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile and your website need to point at each other consistently — same business name, same phone number, same address details. If they're mismatched, or the profile isn't linked to the site at all, Google has a harder time confirming they belong to the same business, which affects how much it trusts either one in local results.

Missing schema markup

Schema is a bit of code that tells Google directly what your business is, where it operates, and what it does — rather than making Google guess from the page text. A site without it can still get indexed, but it's working with less information than a competitor's site that has it set up properly.

Not enough content for Google to rank

A single homepage with a paragraph about the business and a contact form doesn't give Google much to work with. Dedicated pages for each service, and for each area you actually work in, give Google specific things to match against specific searches. A site covering five suburbs with one page per suburb has a real advantage over a site with just one generic "service areas" line.

Slow load times

Page speed is a ranking factor, and it's also a visitor factor — most people won't wait more than a couple of seconds for a site to load before giving up. A slow site can hurt both how often Google shows it and how many people stick around once they land on it.

Questions worth asking

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take for a new website to show up on Google?+
If it's been submitted through Search Console properly, it can be indexed within days. Ranking well for competitive local searches takes longer and depends on more than just being indexed.
Does having a Google Business Profile mean my website will rank too?+
No — they're separate. A strong Google Business Profile can get you into the local map results without your website ranking at all, which is part of why the two get confused.
Do I need a page for every suburb I work in?+
Not every suburb, but dedicated pages for your main service areas give Google far more to match against local searches than one generic page ever will.
Can I fix this myself without rebuilding the site?+
Often, yes. Submitting to Search Console, adding schema, and fixing NAP consistency between your site and Google Business Profile are all fixable without a redesign.
