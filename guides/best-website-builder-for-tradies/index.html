What is the best website builder for a tradie business in Australia?

Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, Google Sites, and done-for-you — a fair look at who each one actually suits.

Here's the short of it

There isn't one single best option

There isn't one best option — it depends on your budget, how comfortable you are building something yourself, and how much your own time is worth. Wix suits beginners who want the most guided experience. Squarespace suits people who want a better-looking result out of the box. WordPress suits anyone who wants full control and doesn't mind a learning curve. Google Sites is free but limited, and not well suited to generating leads. A done-for-you build suits anyone who'd rather spend that time on paying work.

The gist

Key takeaways

✓Each builder genuinely suits a different type of person — there's no universal "best," despite what most comparison articles claim.
✓Wix is the most beginner-friendly; Squarespace tends to produce a more polished result with less effort; WordPress offers the most control at the cost of a steeper learning curve.
✓Google Sites is free but limited — workable as a placeholder, not built for converting visitors into calls.
✓Pricing on all of these changes often and gets presented inconsistently between USD and AUD — check the vendor's own current pricing before deciding.
✓The cost that's rarely factored in is your own time — an evening spent in a page builder is an evening not spent quoting jobs.

Wix

Wix is built for people who've never made a website before. The editor is drag-and-drop, templates are ready to go, and it holds your hand through most of the setup. The trade-off is that the result can look and feel like a template unless you spend real time customising it, and moving away from Wix later — if you ever want to — isn't as simple as exporting your content elsewhere.

Squarespace

Squarespace's templates generally look more considered out of the box than Wix's, which matters if you don't have design instincts of your own. It's a bit less beginner-guided than Wix, so expect a slightly steeper first hour or two learning the editor. For a trade business wanting a clean, professional look without much fuss, it's a solid pick.

WordPress

WordPress gives you the most control of any option here — plugins, themes, full ownership of the code if you're self-hosting. That flexibility comes with a real learning curve, and a self-hosted WordPress site also means you're responsible for your own hosting, security, and updates, which is exactly the kind of ongoing work that gets missed. Worth reading our guide on what should be included in a website package before going down this path, so you know what you're taking on.

Google Sites

Google Sites is free and about as simple as it gets, which makes it fine as a temporary placeholder. It's not built for converting visitors into leads — limited design control, no real SEO tools, and it tends to look exactly like what it is. Reasonable as a stopgap, not a long-term answer for a trade business trying to generate work.

Done-for-you

A done-for-you build removes the building part entirely — a website designer handles design, copy, and setup, and you just review the result. This is the option most directly compared against DIY in our DIY vs. hiring a local web designer guide, which covers the fuller trade-off in detail.

The cost nobody prices: your own time

Every comparison of these builders focuses on the monthly fee and skips the actual time cost. Building a site properly — layout, copy, testing on mobile, connecting a domain — takes most first-timers somewhere between 15 and 30 hours. For a tradie, that's hours not spent quoting, not spent on tools, not spent on the job in front of you. It's worth putting a number on your own hourly rate before assuming the cheapest builder is actually the cheapest option. Our full breakdown of this trade-off is in the website cost guide.

Questions worth asking

Frequently asked questions

Is Wix or Squarespace better for a trade business?+
Wix is more beginner-friendly; Squarespace tends to look more polished with less effort. Neither is definitively better — it depends on how much you want to customise versus how much guidance you want.
Is WordPress too complicated for a small trade business?+
It's more capable than most trade businesses need, and the learning curve is real. If you're comfortable with some technical setup, it offers the most flexibility. If not, it's usually more than necessary.
Is Google Sites good enough for a tradie business?+
Fine as a free, temporary placeholder. Not well suited to generating leads or ranking well in local search over the long term.
How much time does building a website myself actually take?+
Most first-timers spend 15–30 hours getting a DIY site to a genuinely finished state, spread across layout, copy, mobile testing, and setup.
