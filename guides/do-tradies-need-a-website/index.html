Does a tradie actually need a website, or is word of mouth enough?

A straight answer on when a website is worth it — and when it genuinely isn't.

Here's the short of it

Is word of mouth actually enough?

For a small number of trade businesses, no — a fully-booked, referral-only operation with no plans to grow doesn't need one. For everyone else, word of mouth still starts the job, but the person who was referred to you now checks you out online before they ring, and that's the step where a website either helps you close the job or quietly loses it.

The gist

Key takeaways

✓A website genuinely isn't necessary for every trade business — it depends on how you get work, not just what trade you're in.
✓Referrals still generate the lead — the website's job happens after, when the person checks you out before calling.
✓A Google Business Profile covers some of this, but not all of it — the gap is where jobs get lost.
✓The decision comes down to whether you're relying on people finding you cold, or just confirming you're legitimate after a referral.

When a website genuinely isn't worth it

If you're running a small operation fed entirely by referrals and repeat clients, fully booked with a waitlist, and not looking to grow — a website probably isn't going to change much for you. Same goes if you're a couple of years from retiring and winding the business down rather than building it up. In both cases, the time and cost of a website is better spent elsewhere, and there's no shame in skipping it.

Where word of mouth quietly needs backup

Here's the part that catches most trade businesses out. A mate refers you to their neighbour. The neighbour doesn't call straight away — they Google your name or your business first, the way most people do before ringing a tradesman they haven't used. If there's nothing there, or what's there looks dated or unfinished, that referral can quietly go cold. The word of mouth still worked — it got you into consideration. What happens next, the checking-you-out step, is where a website earns its keep.

What a Google Business Profile does — and doesn't — cover

A Google Business Profile gives someone your reviews, your hours, your phone number, and a map pin. That covers a lot of the "is this a real business" check. What it doesn't cover well is showing your actual work, explaining what you specialise in, or giving someone a reason to pick you over the next tradie with a similar star rating. That's the gap a website fills. We've written a full breakdown of this in Google Business Profile vs. a website: what does a tradie actually need if you want the detail on how the two work together.

Questions worth asking

Frequently asked questions

Can a tradie business survive on referrals alone without a website?+
Some can, especially small, fully-booked operations with no growth plans. It's less common than it used to be, since even referral-driven leads tend to check online before calling.
Is a Google Business Profile enough on its own?+
It covers reviews, hours, and location well, but it doesn't show your work or explain what makes you the right choice — that's what a website adds.
How do I know if I actually need a website?+
If most of your work comes from repeat clients and referrals and you're not trying to grow, you probably don't need one urgently. If you want more work, or you suspect referrals are going cold before they call, it's worth having.
Does a website help if I'm already fully booked?+
Less urgently — but circumstances change, and a website is easier to have in place before you need it than to build in a hurry once work dries up.
